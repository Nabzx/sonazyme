# 2. Why shape is everything

A protein starts as a chain. There are twenty kinds of amino acid, and a
protein is a particular sequence of them, a few hundred long in the enzymes we
care about. Written down it looks like a long word: `MKTAYIAKQR...`, one letter
per amino acid.

That chain does not stay a line. It folds up, on its own, into one particular
three-dimensional shape, and it folds into very nearly the same shape every
time for a given sequence.

## Why it folds

Each amino acid has a different side group hanging off the chain. Some are
greasy and avoid water, some carry a positive or negative charge, some can
form weak bonds with others, and two of them can form an actual chemical bond
to each other. Drop that chain into water and it rearranges until it finds a
comfortable arrangement:

- **Greasy side groups** get buried in the middle, away from the water, which
  forms the protein's **core**. This is the single biggest force holding a
  protein together.
- **Hydrogen bonds**, weak individually, form in large numbers along and
  across the chain. They hold the regular local patterns, spirals called
  **helices** and flat strips called **sheets**.
- **Ionic bonds**, also called salt bridges, form between a positive and a
  negative side group that end up near each other.
- **Disulfide bridges** are proper covalent bonds between two cysteine amino
  acids, the strongest link in the list, and comparatively rare.

The finished shape is held by hundreds of these interactions, most of them
individually weak. That matters for the next page.

## The shape is the tool

The active site is not a separate part bolted on. It is formed by whichever
amino acids happen to be brought together by the folding, often from parts of
the chain that are far apart in the sequence. The pocket exists only because
the fold exists.

So: sequence decides the fold, the fold makes the pocket, the pocket does the
work. Break the fold and you do not have a slightly worse enzyme, you have no
enzyme at all.

That chain of consequences is the reason this project exists. We cannot buy an
enzyme and hope. We have to make sure its fold survives where we are sending
it.
