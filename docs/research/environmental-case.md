# How we would quantify the environmental case

- Date: 2026-11-14
- Status: open question, nothing measured

## Why this note exists

The environmental argument for enzymatic biofilm control is easy to assert and
hard to support. Plenty of projects assert it. This note says what it would
take to support it, so that when a number eventually appears in this repo it
arrives with a method behind it rather than a guess.

Until then the README states the reasons to want this and states that none of
them are quantified.

## The four claims, and what each would need

### 1. Less biocide into discharge

**Claim.** An enzyme that removes the matrix reduces how much biocide a loop
needs, so less of it leaves in blowdown.

**What it needs.** Dosing records from a real loop before and after, over
months, not a single treatment. The comparison that matters is total biocide
mass per unit of cooling delivered, because a quieter loop and a cleaner loop
look the same on a dose sheet otherwise.

**Who has the data.** The operator. Not obtainable without a partner.

### 2. Lower impact of what is dosed

**Claim.** An enzyme is a protein, which degrades to amino acids, rather than
a persistent biocide.

**What it needs.** Named enzyme, named formulation. The enzyme itself is the
easy part of this argument; stabilisers and carriers in a real formulation are
the part that needs checking. The honest framing is that this looks
favourable and has not been assessed.

### 3. Better heat transfer, so less energy

**Claim.** A clean heat exchanger surface moves heat better, so the plant
works less hard for the same cooling.

**What it needs.** Thermal resistance across a fouled surface and the same
surface cleaned, measured on a representative rig, and the corresponding
change in pump and chiller work. This is the one claim that could be
quantified on a bench without a wet-lab enzyme at all, by fouling and cleaning
a test surface by any means.

### 4. Longer equipment life

**Claim.** Less microbially influenced corrosion means fewer replacements, and
replacement has an embodied carbon cost.

**What it needs.** Long timescales, and separating microbial corrosion from
every other corrosion mechanism in the loop. The hardest of the four to
support and the easiest to overclaim. We do not make this claim.

## What we can say now

That current practice is chemical, that an enzyme acts on the film rather than
on everything in the water, that a protein degrades where a biocide can
persist, and that cleaner surfaces move heat better. Each of those is a reason
to investigate, and none of them is a measured saving.

## Recommendation

No figure, percentage or cost saving enters the README, the dashboard or a
pitch until one of the four has a measurement behind it. If a figure is wanted
for context, it is an industry figure, cited, and labelled as describing
current practice rather than our effect on it.
