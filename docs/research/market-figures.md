# Market figures, and what each one is allowed to say

- Date: 2026-11-14
- Issue: #39

The market section cites figures that describe the industry, never an effect
of ours. Every number below carries its source and its caveat, and the
dashboard shows the caveat beside the number.

## Liquid cooling, and direct to chip

Forecasts differ widely between publishers, so the honest presentation is a
range rather than a single figure. For 2026 the data-centre liquid cooling
market is put at about USD 4.07 billion by MarketsandMarkets, about USD 5.8
billion by PS Market Research and about USD 6 billion by Global Market
Insights, with growth rates from roughly 18 to 31 per cent a year.

Direct to chip specifically is put at about USD 3.33 billion in 2026 by
MarketsandMarkets, reaching USD 17.31 billion by 2032.

On adoption, PS Market Research estimates that 20 to 25 per cent of AI and
hyperscale sites currently use direct-to-chip cooling, and that about 30 per
cent of AI-optimised sites built between 2024 and 2025 included it in the
base design.

**Caveat, shown with the figure.** The 2026 estimates differ by roughly 45 per
cent between the lowest and the highest, and the adoption percentages come
from a single market research firm rather than from operator data.

## What we may not say

- No saving figure of ours, in per cent or in money, until something has been
  measured. `docs/research/environmental-case.md` sets out what measuring it
  would take.
- No treatment-cost figure per site until a partner gives us one. Published
  water treatment spend varies so widely by site, water source and discharge
  regime that a single number would mislead.
- No fouling-attributable energy figure until we have one with a method
  behind it. The heat-transfer argument is sound in principle and unquantified
  by us.
